## Timeline

### Era I — The Age of Tre Illium *(Ancient)*

- **Kaela Shadowsong**, **Myxtiferious**, and **Sibling Thorne** form Tre Illium — a coalition seeking a lunar balance across Le'Choix.
- Myxtiferious is awarded *Lux Invicta* to rule, vowing never to return to his draconic form; he settles and starts a family.
- Kaela Shadowsong — knowing a Shadowsong in the public eye isn't safe — retreats to the sea, where she meets **Plumbodian the 1st**. The Orb enters the Plumbodian bloodline here.
- Kaela and Myxtiferious have a child together: **Sylphie**.
- Sibling Thorne theologically touches **Virelle**, imbuing her with light powers — meant for her first of kin, though some leak through to Virelle directly.

---

### Era II — The Fall of Tre Illium *(Ancient–Historical)*

- Tre Illium is overthrown by the Shadowsong. The power vacuum allows the **Division of Lumination** to rise as the only organised opposition.
- **Plumbodian the 3rd** is alive during this overthrow.
- Sibling Thorne acts as the Angel of Light — the **Vassal of Light** — to stop the Shadowsong from corrupting La'Choix. He succeeds, but at great personal cost.
- After his efforts, Sibling Thorne has a child with a human woman: **Crecelia Fenrik**.

---

### Era III — Generations of Consequence *(Historical)*

- **Crecelia Fenrik** struggles to raise a demi-god son and leaves **Fenrik** with the Northern Star of the Primordial Fist, believing they'll raise him well.
- **Plumbodian the 4th** undergoes reincarnation through childbirth — the mother dies in labour, the immense stress triggers his rebirth as Plumbodian the 5th. He enrolls the child (**Wizzleforth**) in Cleric classes under the Division of Lumination, as protection — not indoctrination.
- **Sylphie** — long-lived and ancient — eventually settles with **Fenrik**.
- The **Northern Star of the Primordial Fist** (secretly funded by the Division to topple the Royal Dragon Regency once held by Myxtiferious) breaks up Sylphie and Fenrik. When Fenrik tries to leave the order, they go after his wife and children.

---

### Era IV — The Recent Past *(Within Living Memory)*

- **Wizzleforth** attends a Cleric retreat at Glimmerdeep Caverns and shares one night with a woman named **Virelle**. He never sees her again.
- Virelle discovers she is pregnant. Using her magical attunement, she tracks the father through the genetic magic weave — tracing back to Plumbodian the 4th.
- Learning of Plumbodian's carelessness, Virelle acts: she **sinks the Armada of Glimmerdeep**, implants **false memories** into Plumbodian (convincing him he had a family lost with the ships), and disappears — leaving peacefully, untraceable.
- **Elaris** is born. She is sun-blessed — an Aasimar — her powers drawn directly from Sibling Thorne through Virelle's imbued light. She grows up believing her magic comes from her gifted mother. She does not know her father is Wizzleforth.

## The Greyveil Suppression

# The Greyveil Suppression

**Type:** Historical Event  
**Perpetrator:** Order of the Sun (OOTS) — Division of Lumination  
**Location:** Solvetra East (formerly known as Greyveil)  
**Status:** Buried — not part of official record

---

## Overview

The Greyveil Suppression was the forced clearing of Solvetra East, then a thriving common district known as Greyveil. Under the guise of civic restructuring, the Order of the Sun conscripted Dawnbringers and hedge knight families to remove the district's residents at swordpoint. The civilians were unarmed. The OOTS held back and let the conscripts go in first.

The district was subsequently renamed, rebuilt, and reoriented around Order-aligned institutions. The history of what it was — and what was done to take it — has been systematically removed from public record.

---

## What Was Lost

Greyveil was a mixed, community-run district. Open markets, shared institutions, a population that included races and cultures the Order considered undesirable. Much of what the party now sees in Solvetra East — the sanitised buildings, the Order's institutional presence, the purged library shelves — is the replacement layer built over what was erased.

---

## Known Participants

- **Silvanus Shadowfoot** — conscripted Dawnbringer. Followed orders. Has never forgiven himself. The Suppression was the first fracture in his faith; the later ambush on his family completed it. See [Silvanus Shadowfoot](../NPCs/Allies/Silvanus%20Shadowfoot.md).
- **Frah'nk Frapper** — conscripted alongside Silvanus. The two met during the Suppression. It was the defining event that drove Frank away from the Order entirely. See [Frah'nk Frapper](../Players/Characters/Frah'nk%20Frapper.md).

---

## Current State

The name Greyveil does not appear in any official Solvetra record. Citizens alive today either do not know the history or have had it papered over. The Memory Archivist's Vault in Solvetra Intermellum may hold something — and the purged stacks of the Index of Absence in Solvetra East almost certainly did before the Order cleared them.

## Identity — What kind of city is this?

The city of Ars Lapis is one of mining and fine physically good ownership and trading. Where the grit of trade is happening, is likely where Ars Lapis is. Ars Lapis is primarly known for their gem trade. These gems are attuned to light magic and are able to both provide and suck the light out of space, acting as a form of power source for the nation.

It's a thriving community circulated around the trade of "Lumina Gems" and their usage. As mentioned, there is also trade of fine/rare goods, so naturally there are many wealthy people here - but there is also quite an urban sprawl of those trying to cash in on this circular-economy.

Architecture is fine, but a little comtemporary as the town's basis is off the back of the demand of Lumina Gems. The scale, it's the 2nd largest pop. count in Le'Choix, but probably takes up the most space due to the mining operations. The vibe is that of New York mixed with a "hustler's vibe"; everyone is trying to be succesful and make it big, especially those in the urban circle and the outskirts.

Solvetra is a place where people live; Ars Lapis is a place where people succeed - and usually, at any cost. It's not a theives's den, but it's like the top floor of a Stock Exchange where the ethically questionable handshakes happen.

Everyone knows about Ars Lapis and it's 3 "Gem Families" as they call it; the families who have profited off the Gem Trade generationally and contributed to the soaring growth of the area. More in "The Gem Families" (Factions). They obviously too know about the Lumina Gem harvesting and the ethical issues with how miners are treated down there, in Echo Vains.

It's particular a city where people want to be in, but it's one they know they have to be in if they have a commodity or an asset worth selling. If you're looking for the casual, slow-paced lifetyle - Ars Lapis is the wrong place. Everyone is looking to sell, and investors are looking to profit, profit, profit!

People who end up here have nothing to offer unless by intention, and contribute to nothing Ars Lapis-wide. This strip of town is called "Bankrupt Promenade".

# Session 7 — Consequence Framework
_The vault heist is the pivot point of the first arc. Everything before it built to this. Everything after it flows from it. Answer these questions before you run it._

---

## What's Actually At Stake

Before mapping outcomes, be clear on the layers:

- **Adrion's debt** — 5,000gp to Lucien Morvault in 5 nights. Failure to pay = Mass Polymorph, then death.
- **The Shadowbringers' war chest** — the vault holds DOL funds. Draining it cripples the Division's finances in Solvetra.
- **The DOL's symbolic power** — the vault *is* the Division's control over citizens. Robbing it is a public act of defiance whether the party wants it to be or not.
- **The party's cover** — after the Lumina Lab, the DOL already know these people exist. The vault heist ends that ambiguity entirely.

---

## The Four Outcomes

### A — Full Success
The party gets the gold, pays Lucien, the Shadowbringers get their cut, nobody dies, nobody is identified.

- Does the DOL know it was robbed immediately, or is there a window of confusion?
- How does the DOL explain the missing funds to the citizens whose money it was?
- Does the Lorewarden Emeritus respond personally — and what does that look like?
- Does Silas get what he needed from this, or does the Shadowbringer war chest change his plans in a way the party didn't expect?
- What does Lucien Morvault do when he's paid — does he just leave, or does getting his money reveal something about *why* he wanted it and what the "agreement" with Adrion actually was?

---

### B — Partial Success
They get some gold — enough for Lucien, not enough to cripple the DOL, or vice versa. Or they get in and out but leave a trail.

- If they can only do one — pay Lucien or fund the Shadowbringers — which do they choose, and what does the unchosen consequence look like?
- If they leave a trail (a witness, a dead guard, Traelensch rats them out), how quickly does the DOL identify the party by name?
- If Vanya Windros' key isn't recovered, what happens to that 4th lock? Does the vault stay partially sealed?
- Does a partial win feel like a win to the Shadowbringers, or does Silas' trust in the party take a hit?

---

### C — Heist Fails, Party Escapes
They don't get the gold but get out alive. Now they have the debt and no plan.

- What does Lucien Morvault actually do when the deadline passes — does he follow through on Mass Polymorph, or does he want something else from Adrion?
- Does Zatharial see an opportunity here? Adrion's life is in danger — the contract becomes relevant. When and how does Zatharial resurface?
- Does Silas cut the party loose, or does he double down because they're still the best asset he has?
- Is there an alternative path to 5,000gp that opens up — a job, a mark, an NPC who'd pay for something the party could do?

---

### D — Catastrophic Failure
Captured, killed, or publicly exposed. The DOL now has the party by name, face, and affiliation.

- If the party is captured — what does the DOL do with them? Execute them publicly (too messy)? Interrogate them (what do they know)? Use them as leverage against the Shadowbringers?
- If they escape but are publicly named as enemies of the Order — what changes in Solvetra? Can they walk the streets? Does anyone shelter them?
- Does Silas sacrifice the party to protect the broader rebellion, or does he burn resources to get them out?
- What does this do to the Shadowbringers' position in Solvetra — are they exposed too, or does the DOL not know the connection yet?

---

## Villain Threads — Where Are They After This?

### The DOL & The Lorewarden Emeritus
- Valerius is dead. His replacement was "already organised." Does the heist accelerate that replacement's arrival?
- L.E. signed the Lumina Elixir distribution map. He holds the 4th vault key. Has he been in Solvetra this whole time, watching? Or is this the event that brings him here personally?
- What does the Lorewarden *actually want* — permanent rule through light, yes, but what's the personal motivation underneath that? Power? Fear? Belief?
- Is there a moment where the party could meet him before a confrontation — and what would he say to them?

### Lucien Morvault
- The debt is framed as "as we agreed upon" — Adrion signed something he didn't read, but this predates Zatharial's contract. What *is* the original agreement?
- Is Lucien connected to the DOL, the Shadowbringers, or neither?
- If paid — does he stay in play, or disappear? If he stays, what does he want next?
- If not paid — does Mass Polymorph happen immediately, or is there a scene first? What does Lucien look like in person?

### Zatharial
- He signed Adrion up to give away the party's souls on Adrion's death. He said he has a vested interest in them defeating the Light Vassal — so he doesn't *want* them dead yet.
- If the heist goes badly and Adrion is at risk of dying, Zatharial appears. What does he offer? What does he want in return?
- Does the rest of the party know about the contract? If not — is this the session it comes out?
- Zatharial said he'd use the contract to "split a wedge down the middle." What's the wedge — which two party members are most at odds, and how does he exploit that?

### The Northern Star of the Primordial Fist
- They sent goons to Solvetra Meadows to kill Fenrik. Those goons are dead.
- Aurevon Beitomy Erstua ordered the hit. Does Aurevon escalate — send someone better, come personally, or try a different approach entirely?
- The party is now far louder than they were at the Meadows. Does the NSPF's increased profile on the party make them *more* aggressive or *more* cautious?
- Is there a moment during or after the heist where Fenrik realises the NSPF has found them again?

### The Goblin Settlement
- The party killed Boss Dragg and every goblin in the Lumina Lab. Word reaches the settlement.
- Does the revenge arc activate *during* the heist (worst possible timing), *after* it (when the party is already exposed), or is it a slow burn into Ars Lapis?
- What does the settlement want — blood, or something more specific (a body returned, a debt owed, an object taken)?

These are the cracks. The heist pressure-tests all of them.

- How much does the party actually know about Adrion's past at this point?
- Is this the session where something comes out — the contract, the debt's origin, the village — or does it stay buried one more arc?
- If the party learns about the soul contract mid-heist, what happens to the plan?

### Kaelen Has Valerius' Form
- Kaelen left Session 6 with the ability to disguise as Valerius du Noirveil.
- Valerius is dead — but no one outside the party knows that yet (presumably).
- How far can this be pushed? Does "Valerius" still have authority in the vault, or has his disappearance already been noted?
- What happens when Valerius' replacement arrives and the party is still using the face?

### Plumbodian's Lorewarden Quest
- The Lorewarden gave Plumbodian a mission back in Session 5. Plumbodian was reminded of it by note in Session 6.
- What *is* that quest — and is it in conflict with robbing the vault the Lorewarden effectively controls?
- Does completing the quest require Plumbodian to betray the party, or just keep a secret?
- The Lorewarden holds the 4th vault key. Does Plumbodian know this?

### Wizzleforth's Growing Understanding
- He's been reading DOL texts, gained Religion proficiency, and experienced what sounds like an astral projection of his own lineage in Session 5.
- Does he know yet that Elaris exists? That Virelle sank ships to protect herself from being found?
- Is there something in the vault — a record, a name, a document — that could crack his understanding of his own family open?

---

## Ripple Effects on the World

### If the DOL Loses the Vault
- Citizens' money was seized for DOL use. If the vault is robbed, does any of that money make it back to citizens? Does the Shadowbringer plan include redistribution, or is it purely tactical?
- How does the DOL fund operations in Solvetra without the vault? Do they pull resources from Ars Lapis? Does that weaken them elsewhere?
- Does losing the vault *publicly* — even if the DOL tries to suppress it — inspire other Shadowbringer cells to act?

### The Lumina Elixir
- Production in Solvetra is destroyed. The distribution map pointed to Ars Lapis.
- Is there a secondary production facility? Is Ars Lapis already receiving elixir from somewhere else?
- At some point the world finds out what the elixir is made from. Is the heist the moment something gets published, leaked, or nailed to a door?

### The Echo Unheard
- Måneklang revealed this name at the False Stage. The bards there were part of Ne'leak Shadowsong's bardic cult.
- Nothing has followed up on this yet. Is there a moment in the heist — or its aftermath — where this resurfaces?
- What *is* the Echo Unheard, and when do you want the party to start asking the right questions about it?

---

## The Question Underneath Everything

The party are, knowingly or not, the heirs of Tre Illium's unfinished project — a lunar balance that no one has championed in generations.

- Do any of them know this yet?
- Is the vault heist the moment one of them gets a glimpse — a document, a symbol, a name they recognise — that suggests their involvement in this is not coincidental?
- What's the earliest possible moment you want them to feel like they are part of something *older* than the Shadowbringers vs. DOL fight?

# Ars Lapis

A fortress-city built to guard the region's most coveted resource: its deep-veined mines of precious gems. The city's name — Lapis, meaning "stone" — is both literal and symbolic. Everything in Ars Lapis is carved from permanence: its towering walls, its rigid hierarchy, and its unyielding devotion to extraction.

Governed by a monopolistic autocracy, power is concentrated among a few elite mining houses sanctioned by the Division of Lumination. These families control not only the gem trade, but the lives of the citizens who toil beneath the earth. Most inhabitants are miners, labourers, or support workers, bound by generational contracts and the weight of tradition.

Despite its harsh structure, Ars Lapis is revered for its fortified beauty. Its castle, with many towers, stands as both a symbol of protection and control. Trade is tightly regulated and outsiders are viewed with suspicion unless bearing sanctioned contracts or rare offerings.

### Lumina Elixir
_Loot & Magic Items/In Circulation/Lumina Elixir.md_

# Lumina Elixir

**Type:** Alchemical Substance — Distributed  
**Origin:** Lumina Lab, Solvetra West  
**Status:** Production facility destroyed (Session 6)

---

## Overview

A Division of Lumination-produced elixir, manufactured covertly beneath Solvetra West. Its true composition was concealed behind the legitimate-looking Lumina Lab alchemist shop above ground.

---

## Composition

- Worg saliva (extracted through abuse)
- Worg tears
- Worg blood
- Boiled and filtered river water
- Secret herb mix

The Worgs were kept captive and beaten to force salivation. The distillation process ran across multiple lab floors before bottling.

---

## Distribution

A distribution map found in the lab indicated a planned rollout across **Ars Lapis**. The map was signed *L.E.* — likely referencing Lorewarden Emeritus.

---

## Effects

**Lumina Elixir** is engineered as a creativity stimulant — a "spark in a bottle."

**Low/moderate doses:**
- Induces a state of vivid, hyper-lucid dreaming — even while awake. Ideas arrive fully-formed, connections that shouldn't exist suddenly do, and the user feels touched by genius.
- Short-term boost to creative output and insight.
- Subtle "too clear" aftertaste, and a faint lingering glow behind the eyes.

**Repeated/prolonged use:**
- The vividness doesn't fade — it accumulates. Dreams bleed into waking thought until the user can no longer separate the two.
- Eventually tips into a permanent fog: a slack-jawed, glassy-eyed "blank stare" — the mind still lit up inside, but unable to surface anything coherent. No resistance, no complaints. Just gone.
- This end-stage matches the "blank look" Frah'nk associates with DOL pacification elsewhere — same substance, same outcome, different delivery method.

The Fountain of Dreams (Solvetra West) may have been an early, diluted field test of this exact effect — explaining the "too clear" water and Wizzleforth's vivid dreaming after drinking from it.

---

## Notes

- Distribution map indicates the elixir is being shipped onward to **Ars Lapis**
- Production was overseen by goblin labour under DOL direction via Valerius du Noirveil
- A filtration system labelled *"Stage III: Salivary Infusion Chamber"* was documented in lab blueprints

# Zatharial

**Type:** Devil  
**Status:** Active — contract void, still in play

---

## Overview

A devil who approached Adrion in Solvetra West (Session 4), offering to teleport him to the party in exchange for signing a contract. Adrion signed without reading it. When Adrion disappeared, Zatharial appeared before the party and theatrically tore the contract in front of them — making clear he operates on his own terms, not theirs.

Inspiration: Raphael from Baldur's Gate 3. Charming, theatrical, unhurried. He doesn't threaten — he presents options. He always seems to be doing the party a favour, even when he isn't. He finds the whole situation delightful.

---

## The Contract (Voided)

The original contract granted Zatharial the right to claim the party's souls upon Adrion's death. With Adrion gone, Zatharial tore it up publicly — a power move, not a concession. He wants the party to know he *chose* to let them go. For now.

---

## Zatharial's Interest

He believes the party are destined to defeat the Light Vassal. His interest in them is genuine but entirely self-serving — he wants the Light Vassal gone, and these are the people who can do it. He will help, hinder, and manipulate as it suits him, always with a smile.

He does not work for the DOL or the Shadowbringers. He operates on a longer timeline than either.

---

## How to Play Him

- Always composed, always a step ahead — or performing like he is
- Never raises his voice; the quieter he gets, the more dangerous he is
- Frames everything as a gift or an opportunity: *"I'm not asking for much — consider it a favour between friends"*
- Tears up the contract in front of the party not to free them, but to remind them that he *could have* collected and chose not to — they owe him something now, even if nothing was signed
- Will reappear at dramatically inconvenient moments with a new proposition

---

## Notes

- His involvement is not tied to the DOL or Shadowbringers — he operates independently
- The party knows he exists and that a contract existed; they saw it destroyed
- He has not made a new ask yet — but he will

# Frah'nk Frapper

**Age:** 33  
**Height:** 6'3"  
**Origin:** Wealthy hedge knight family — Order of Light  
**Affiliation:** Shadowbringers  
**Introduction:** Friend of Du'geshk Maharjuuj; sent to find the party by Silvanus Shadowfoot

---

## Background

Frah'nk was born into a hedge knight family with nominal ties to the Order of Light — comfortable, respected, but never truly powerful. His mother died in childbirth. His father raised him with the Order's values: duty, hierarchy, obedience. Frank never took to it. He could follow a rule he understood, but not one that existed simply because it always had.

His father was killed in battle when Frank was around 17. Shortly after, Frank was conscripted into **The Greyveil Suppression** — the OOTS-ordered clearing of what is now Solvetra East. At the time it was a thriving common district: open markets, mixed population, community-run. The residents were unarmed. Frank and the other conscripted Dawnbringers were told it was civic restructuring. They were sent in first. What followed was a forced removal at swordpoint — civilians, not combatants. The Order's proper forces watched from behind. Frank survived. The district didn't.

It was not a battle. There was no enemy. The Order called it progress. Frank called it what it was, walked away from his estate, his title, and everything he couldn't carry, and took his father's money and disappeared.

It was here that he met **Silvanus Shadowfoot** — also conscripted, also from a hedge knight family, also watching the same thing with the same expression. The friendship that formed that day has lasted since.

---

## The Sellsword Years

For the better part of a decade Frank worked as a mercenary — taking contracts across different cities, cultures, and factions. He was never picky about the job as long as it wasn't pointless, and he made a point of working with people the Order looked down on. Half-orcs, goblins, outcasts. He learned languages, customs, and perspectives that the hedge knight world had no interest in.

The more he saw of the world, the worse the Order looked. He witnessed a second DOL action — this one quieter, no battle, just enforcement. A town that had been openly trading lunar artefacts was visited by Order envoys. Within a fortnight, the market was gone, three people had vanished, and the rest had that familiar blank look Frank would later learn to associate with the Lumina Elixir. No resistance. No noise. Just erasure.

That was when he stopped being indifferent and started being angry.

---

## The Shadowbringers & Magic

Frank found the Shadowbringers through a contact in a city between jobs — or, more accurately, they found him. His reputation for taking the dirty work nobody else wanted had preceded him.

It was within the Shadowbringers that Frank first encountered runic magic — a pre-DOL form of spellworking that the Order had systematically suppressed and mislabelled as blasphemous. Where conventional magic flows through study and structure, Frank's magic is worked through marks — carved, drawn, burned. It doesn't look like the magic the Division teaches. It looks older, rougher, and wrong to anyone trained in the Order's doctrine. Frank took to it instinctively, possibly because it operates on a logic he can actually understand: cause, mark, effect. No authority required.

---

## Silvanus Shadowfoot

Frank's closest bond within the Shadowbringers is with **Silvanus Shadowfoot** — Silas' father, the former Oathbreaker Paladin who lost his leg, his wife, and his daughter to a thieving ambush and now walks the Path of the Shadowbringer. Two men shaped by what the Order did and didn't do for their families. The friendship is low-key and unspoken in the way old grievances tend to be — they don't need to explain themselves to each other.

It was Silvanus who recently sent Frank a letter. The message was simple: a group of adventurers had found their way into Silas' orbit, caused the kind of trouble that gets noticed, and Silvanus wanted someone he trusted to meet them. Not babysit — assess. Make sure they're the real thing and not a liability that'll get his son killed.

Frank also knows **Du'geshk Maharjuuj** from overlapping Shadowbringer operations — they've worked jobs together and have a mutual, no-nonsense respect.

---

## Personality

Frank has very little patience for rules that exist for their own sake and no patience at all for institutions that protect themselves at the expense of the people inside them. He doesn't hate the Order because he was taught to — he hates it because he watched it operate and drew his own conclusions.

He takes the hard roles because someone has to and because he's good at it. He doesn't perform sacrifice; he just doesn't see the point in skipping it.

He is not warm in the conventional sense but is reliably honest, which in this world counts for quite a lot.

---

## Notes

- Connected to the party via Du'geshk Maharjuuj (mutual contact, Shadowbringers)
- Silvanus' letter was the direct prompt for him finding the party — he is also quietly there to assess whether they're worth Silas' trust
- His runic magic should feel visually distinct from anything the party has seen — blasphemous-looking by DOL standards, instinctive rather than academic
- The Greyveil Suppression is documented in the Solvetra East location file and Silvanus Shadowfoot's NPC sheet

# PC Family Arcs

The interconnected lineages and backstories behind the party's characters — these threads form the backbone of the campaign's main arc.

---

## The Plumbodian–Wizzleforth–Elaris Line

- **Plumbodian** is Wizzleforth's father, who is in turn Elaris's father.
- **Wizzleforth**, many years ago during a Cleric retreat at Glimmerdeep Caverns, met a woman named **Virelle** and shared a night with her. He did not see her again and does not know what happened.
- **Plumbodian the 3rd** was alive when Tre Illium was overthrown by the Shadowsong. The power vacuum allowed the Division of Lumination to rise as the only organised opposition.
- **Plumbodian the 4th's** reincarnation came about when he had a child — the mother dying in childbirth, the immense stress triggering a rebirth. He enrolled this child (Wizzleforth) in Cleric classes and had him study under the Division of Lumination for protection against a possible second Shadowsong attack. Wizzleforth is therefore relatively normal — this was protection, not indoctrination.

**Virelle's fate:**
Virelle had Wizzleforth's child — Elaris — and searched the lands for the father. Using her attunement to magic, she tracked Plumbodian the 4th via the genetic magic weave of her family. Learning of his carelessness and his drunken ambition to sail, Virelle burst into light, fractured the ocean, and sank all surrounding ships — including **The Armada of Glimmerdeep**. Using the last of her magic, she implanted false memories into Plumbodian (now the 5th) — convincing him he once had a family that went down with the ships, and that he received the Orb through that family. Her motive: to not be tracked, and to leave peacefully after destroying his fleet.

**The Orb:**
The Orb passed to Plumbodian from his mother — **Kaela Shadowsong**, ⅓ of Tre Illium. The Orb is attuned to the gate of Luxfracta, and is the piece required to activate the passthrough to the Realm of Shadowsong. It conduits the magic of the Shadowrealm through solar energy, creating a divine moon presence and establishing perfect harmony.

> *If the Shadowsong obtain the Orb — they destroy it and unleash ultimate power. If the Division/Order obtain it — they seal Lux Infracta and usher in permanent rule through Light. It's a lose-lose if either side gains full control. Tre Illium's goal was a lunar outcome.*

---

## Fenrik's Line — Sibling Thorne & the Primordial Fist

- **Sibling Thorne**, ⅓ of Tre Illium, is the Angel of Light — created out of pure divine solar energy (the Vassal of Light, final boss) to stop the Shadowsong from corrupting La'Choix.
- After his efforts, he had a child with a human woman — **Crecelia Fenrik**. Crecelia had no idea how to raise a demi-god son and left the child with the **Northern Star of the Primordial Fist**, believing they'd raise him well. This did not work out — when Fenrik wanted to leave the order, they came after his wife and children.
- **Fenrik** is therefore the son of a demi-god and carries divine heritage he may not fully understand.
- **Sylphie** (daughter of Myxtiferious and Kaela Shadowsong) had a relationship with Fenrik. The Northern Star of the Primordial Fist broke them up — funded and bankrolled by the Division to topple the Royal Dragon Regency, the power and throne once held by Myxtiferious.

---

## Elaris's Line — The Vassal of Light

- **Virelle** was touched theologically by Sibling Thorne and given light powers. These were meant for her first of kin, but some leaked through to Virelle directly.
- **Elaris**'s patron is Sibling Thorne — she just doesn't know it, or who he is. Imbued with the same mystic and pure light power used to save La'Choix, she believes her magic is inherited from her divinely talented mother. In actuality, it comes straight from the Vassal of Light. Elaris is sun-blessed with powers beyond human/elf comprehension — an Aasimar.

---

## Sylphie's Line — Myxtiferious & Kaela Shadowsong

- **Myxtiferious**, the Red Dragon Lord and ⅓ of Tre Illium, had a relationship with Kaela Shadowsong. Their child is **Sylphie**.
- Myxtiferious was awarded *Lux Invicta* to rule until the end of his bloodline, vowing never to return to his draconic form — this allowed him to settle and start a family.
- **Kaela Shadowsong** knew that a Shadowsong in the public eye — even one who'd turned her back on her lineage — wouldn't be safe. She settled to live out at sea, where there was no discrimination. This is where she met Plumbodian the 1st.
- Sylphie has been alive a very long time, and eventually settled with Fenrik. The NSPF broke them apart.

---

## The Shadowrealm & the Lunar Goal

The realm of Shadowsong locks away the chaotic dark magic used to try to bring about the end of times in La'Choix. The Orb conduits magic from the Shadowrealm through solar energy, creating a divine moon presence and establishing perfect harmony.

Before Tre Illium were overthrown, they were trying to create a **lunar presence** throughout Le'Choix — a perfect symbiosis of night and light that would satisfy all factions with power. The idea lost steam, the citizens forgot, but the symbolism and effigies remain — waiting for someone to champion the idea again.

**That's where the party comes in.**

# The Greyveil Suppression — Story Thread

**Status:** Dormant — available to activate  
**Entry Point:** Frah'nk Frapper joining the party; Solvetra East exploration

## The Hook

The party is now travelling with someone who was there. Frah'nk doesn't bring it up unprompted — it's not something he performs — but if the party spends time in Solvetra East, visits the right buildings, or asks the right questions, the thread opens naturally.

Solvetra East doesn't look like a place that was taken by force. That's the point. It looks clean, institutional, Order-aligned. The violence has been replaced by paperwork and architecture. But the bones of what it was are still there for anyone who knows how to look.

---

## Where the Evidence Lives

**Index of Absence — Solvetra East (Library)**
The shelves were cleared "by decree of the Curator of Memory." The Curator is an Order title. What was removed almost certainly included records of Greyveil — the name, the population, the events. The quest hook is already active: *recover the forbidden texts before the purge completes.* If the party gets there in time, a fragment of what Greyveil was could still exist.

**Memory Archivist's Vault — Solvetra Intermellum**
The Intermellum is where things the Order couldn't fully destroy ended up hidden. The Memory Archivist's Vault is explicitly a repository of things that shouldn't exist. Greyveil records — names, a census, a firsthand account — could be buried here. The Archivist may not even know what they're holding.

**Frah'nk Frapper**
He was there. He can describe it. If the party finds a fragment of evidence and brings it to him, or if they're standing in Solvetra East and he's with them, this is a moment. He doesn't need to be dramatic about it — matter-of-fact is worse.

**Silvanus Shadowfoot**
Also there. Carries more guilt about it than Frank does, or at least wears it differently. If the party confronts him with evidence, or if Frank and Silvanus are in the same room when it comes up, there's something to play there.

---

## What the Party Could Uncover

| Discovery | Source | Impact |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| The name "Greyveil" in a recovered text | Index of Absence | Confirms the district had a prior identity |
| A census or population record | Memory Archivist's Vault | Makes the erasure personal — real names, real people |
| An eyewitness account of the Suppression | Either archive | Corroborates what Frank/Silvanus lived through |

---

## What It Means

The Greyveil Suppression isn't just backstory for Frank and Silvanus. It's proof of something the party has only seen the edges of so far — that the DOL doesn't just suppress and control, it *erases*. Solvetra East is the most complete example of what they're capable of, and it's been sitting in plain sight the whole time.

If the party exposes it — publishes a record, confronts the Order publicly, or gets the story to the right people — it could shift how Solvetra's citizens see the Division. Not just as oppressive, but as the force that destroyed their own city's history and replaced it with a lie.

# Factions of Le'Choix

## Tre Illium (Illuminare, Illuminari)

The three founders of Le Dernier Choix. Operated under strict secrecy — their true identities are still unknown to the general public. Known to have established the region with a vision of lunar-solar balance, but their rule was eventually undermined by both the Shadowsong and the newly forming Division of Lumination.

The three members of Tre Illium:

The ruling autocracy of Le'Choix. Rose to power following the fall of Tre Illium, filling the power vacuum left when the Shadowsong attacked. # World Overview — Le Dernier Choix

## Le Dernier Choix (Le'Choix)

The city name in French, meaning "The Final Choice". This is the region in which the campaign takes place. Founded by a group of three known as "Illuminare, Illuminari" — commonly referred to as "Tre Illium". The actual identities of these founders are unknown, as they operated under guidelines of utmost secrecy.

These conditions created the foundation for those with selfish intent to establish a region-leading autocracy under the **Division of Lumination (DIL)**. The DIL rule over Le'Choix with no real opposition remaining. They aren't necessarily unfair and citizens aren't particularly hard done by — it's simply that the power balance is skewed to the top and is now generational. Le'Choix still faces feudalist-shaped societal issues like any other fantasy land.

---

## Cities & Locations

### Ars Cultura

A city suspended between revelation and secrecy. Known for its radiant architecture and esoteric traditions, Ars Cultura is less a place and more a living archive — where light is studied not merely as illumination, but as a language, a force, and a veil.

Scholars, artists, and arcane theorists dwell in a labyrinth of stone chambers and glowing halls, each etched with symbols whose meanings are lost to time or deliberately obscured. Knowledge here is curated, not shared — passed through generations like heirlooms, often altered, sometimes erased.

At the centre of Ars Cultura's deepest research establishment stands **The Prismic Scar (Luxfracta)** — a structure older than any record, untouched by time yet visibly wounded. It emits a golden light that bends unnaturally, casting shadows that whisper and shimmer with impossible geometry. A jagged tear within its glow reveals a star-filled void — an anomaly that defies all known magical theory. No one knows who built it, why it was scarred, or what lies beyond. Some say it's a wound in reality itself. Others believe it's a test left by the Tre Illium. Most simply avoid it.

---

### Ars Pontaris

A city suspended between two landmasses, both geographically and ideologically. Though once a neutral crossing point, it now serves as a polished showcase for the Division of Lumination's soft power. The city's cultural diversity remains intact on the surface — languages, customs, and aesthetics vary — but beneath it all, the Division has achieved near-total control.

Compliance is not enforced through fear, but through comfort: Ars Pontaris enjoys a higher socioeconomic status than much of the northwest, with clean streets, thriving markets, and well-funded institutions. Most residents see little reason to question the Division's influence, especially when it arrives dressed as prosperity.

Architecture subtly echoes the radiant geometry of La'Choix's capital, festivals celebrate "illumination" as civic pride, and education gently reshapes historical memory. Shadow markets and cryptic traditions persist in quiet corners — tolerated more than feared, curiosities rather than threats.

*A mural in the central plaza shifts subtly at dusk, revealing a hidden sigil linked to Tre Illium. A scholar from the bridge district offers riddles hinting at the true origin of the Division. A festival is approaching — designed by the Division — but local artists plan a subversive counter-performance beneath the bridge.*

---

### Ars Lapis

A fortress-city built to guard the region's most coveted resource: its deep-veined mines of precious gems. The city's name — *Lapis*, meaning "stone" — is both literal and symbolic. Everything in Ars Lapis is carved from permanence: its towering walls, its rigid hierarchy, and its unyielding devotion to extraction.

Governed by a **monopolistic autocracy**, power is concentrated among a few elite mining houses sanctioned by the Division of Lumination. These families control not only the gem trade, but the lives of the citizens who toil beneath the earth. Most inhabitants are miners, labourers, or support workers, bound by generational contracts and the weight of tradition.

Despite its harsh structure, Ars Lapis is revered for its fortified beauty. Its castle, with many towers, stands as both a symbol of protection and control. Trade is tightly regulated and outsiders are viewed with suspicion unless bearing sanctioned contracts or rare offerings.

---

### Mount Mortifera

Mount Mortifera is not a location. It is a question.

When drawn, its proportions warp the parchment. When spoken of, tongues falter. Even its name is suspect — *Mortifera* is not a word in any known dialect of La'Choix. Some believe it was whispered into the minds of early settlers by something beneath the soil. Others claim it was never named, only recognised.

The mountain's presence distorts reality. Light bends unnaturally near its base. Shadows stretch too long. Clocks lose rhythm. Birds refuse to fly over it. The Echo Veins, which pulse with memory and resonance across the continent, go silent in its vicinity — as if the mountain consumes not just sound, but meaning.

Some shards of Luxfracta hum when pointed toward Mortifera, as if yearning for reunion. Tre Illium is said to have vanished near Mortifera's base. Not destroyed — absorbed.

There are no ruins on Mortifera. No shrines. No bones. It is as if the mountain erases history.

---

### The Prismic Scar (Luxfracta)

Luxfracta is known, but not understood. Its name appears in official documents, whispered in bureaucratic circles, and etched into the margins of restricted texts. The general populace accepts its existence the way one accepts gravity — present, powerful, and utterly opaque.

Access to Luxfracta is tightly controlled. It is not displayed, not traded, not studied openly. Even within the Department of Internal Liminality, knowledge is compartmentalised. Those who work near it are often unaware. Those who know are forbidden to speak.

What almost no one knows: Luxfracta is not native to this world. It is what remains of something folded out of time — not a resource. It is a scar. And those who've truly encountered it do not describe it. They change. Or they disappear.

---

### Kageyama (Shadow Mountain / 長山令)

Kageyama clings to the shoulder of Mount Mortifera like a shadow that refuses to be cast away. Its rooftops are filled with red-clay tiles, and its streets wind like veins through stone — quiet, deliberate, and ancient. The air carries the scent of ash and cedar.

Kageyama was built not in defiance of the volcano, but in reverence. The founders believed Mortifera to be the vessel of a slumbering god. They carved their homes into the rock, planted gardens in volcanic soil, and lit lanterns that burn with cold flame — blue and silver, never gold.

Each solstice, the city falls silent. Mirrors are hung from doorways, people wear veiled masks, and prayers are whispered into hollow stones cast into the mountain's breath.

---

### The Palegleam

A forest steeped in lunar resonance and quiet dread. Its outer edges appear almost serene — pine trees swaying under moonlight, nocturnal creatures moving with graceful intent. But as one ventures deeper, the forest begins to shed familiar rhythms. Light behaves strangely. Trees grow pale and brittle, their bark resembling bone or ash.

At the heart of the Palegleam, life feels suspended — neither dead nor alive, caught in a state of lunar stasis. Flora glows faintly with bioluminescent hues, and some plants react to emotion or memory rather than touch.

Legends speak of a forgotten shrine at the centre, tied to the Tre Illium or perhaps to an older, darker force that predates them.

### Solvetra

Solvetra is more town than city — modest in scale, but vast in implication. Its narrow streets and tiered courtyards fold inward like a labyrinth. The population is largely working class, their lives shaped by quiet endurance and unspoken codes.

Though there are no walls or curfews, Solvetra functions as a **passive police state** under the watchful presence of the Division of Lumination. Their agents rarely speak, but their lanterns burn in every district, casting long shadows and longer memories. Locals have learned to navigate by instinct, not maps, and speak in layered idioms that veil truth in metaphor.

### Solvetra Meadows

Solvetra Meadows lies just beyond the reach of the lanternlight — an outskirt of quiet resignation where tension gives way to stillness. Compared to the town proper, it's gentler, slower, and less watched, but also less alive.

People here aren't oppressed so much as forgotten. There's little fear, but even less opportunity. Children grow up knowing the boundaries of their world by heart, and dreams tend to shrink to fit the soil.

*For the characters, it's a beginning — but not one meant to last.*

### Virelle

A border town where lines blur — between nations, cultures, and even the reach of authority. Nestled along the coast, it hums with a gentle defiance: sun-warmed stone, salt-stained markets, and a rhythm of life that favors flavor over fear.

The Division of Lumination holds little sway here. Control, after all, is only necessary for those born-and-bred in Le Dernier Choix. In Virelle, citizenship is fluid and the Division rarely bothers with enforcement. The town thrives on blend rather than clash — its dialects braided, its recipes borrowed and perfected.

### Echo Veins

But beneath the shimmer lies a darker truth — Echo Veins has been mined for generations using unethical labour, its tunnels shaped more by desperation than design.

Today, the upper chambers have been polished into a modest tourist attraction — guides speak of harmonic crystals and ancient acoustics, while carefully avoiding the deeper shafts where the songs grow dissonant. Echo Veins is beautiful, yes — but it remembers.

### Lunarthorn

A quiet enclave nestled deep within the whispering pinewoods, where moonlight filters through the canopy like a benediction. The community is serene, unhurried, and gently insular — not out of hostility, but from a contentment that asks for little beyond the rhythm of the lunar cycle.

The Division of Lumination pays Lunarthorn little mind — its inhabitants are seen as harmless mystics. But beneath the calm lies something older — an ideology not lost, but quietly preserved. Their rituals, symbols, and way of marking time all echo a forgotten framework that once held cosmic truths.

# Documentation — Example Structure

This folder contains example documentation for the DM Cockpit app, structured as a fictional D&D campaign called *Le Dernier Choix*.

The campaign, story, NPCs, locations, and lore within are entirely made up and exist solely to demonstrate how the app's documentation system works in practice — folder hierarchy, markdown formatting, cross-linking between files, and how content surfaces inside the app.

## Structure

```
documentation/
├── History & Lore/         — World history, mysteries, named phenomena
├── Locations/              — Cities, districts, and points of interest
├── Magic Items/
│   └── Distributed/        — Items in active circulation
├── NPCs/                   — Named characters, used and reserved
├── Players/                — Party overview, languages, personal arcs
├── Quests/
│   ├── Active/             — Current quests and objectives
│   └── Completed/          — Resolved quests for reference
├── Rules & Tables/         — Encounter tables, house rules
├── Sessions/               — Session notes and planning templates
├── Shops & Economy/        — Shop inventories and pricing
├── Villains/               — Antagonists and their details
└── World/                  — Factions, overworld overview
```

Any markdown files placed in this folder (or subfolders) will be picked up by the app automatically.
